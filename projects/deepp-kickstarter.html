What separates a funded Kickstarter campaign from one that isn't? An exploratory data analysis of 375K+ campaigns (2009–2017) to identify the characteristics of successful projects — built for a prospective-investor audience.

Problem & Scope

What investors actually need to know

Problem statement

What are the characteristics of successful projects that investors should look for in potential future investments?

Scope

This project focuses on identifying Kickstarter projects from 2009–2017 for potential future investments.

Objectives

  • Provide insights for investors on potential future successful projects and trends.
  • Identify the category with the highest success rate.
  • Identify the impact of backers and goal completion on project success.

Data Analysis

Key findings

Nine findings from the exploratory data analysis, each traced back to the underlying chart in the Tableau dashboard below.

01Success rates held stable from 2009–2013, then declined in 2014–2015, before recovering and stabilizing in 2016–2017.

Stable (2009–2013) ranging from 43% to 47%.
Declining (2014–2015) dropping to 32% in 2014, then 28% in 2015.
Recovering (2016–2017) rising back to 33% and 37%.

02The 2014–2015 decline traces to a drop in both number of backers and amount pledged.

Backers fell to averages of 96 (2014) and 99 (2015).
Pledged fell to averages of $7,935 (2014) and $9,059 (2015).

03The 2016–2017 recovery coincided with a rebound in backer support and pledged amounts.

Backers rose to averages of 124 (2016) and 129 (2017).
Pledged rose to averages of $11,248 (2016) and $12,347 (2017).

04There is a positive correlation between success rate and average number of backers — more backers tends to mean a higher success rate.

Up trends (2009–2011 & 2015–2017) show backers rising alongside success rate.
Down trend (2013–2014) shows both falling together, from 43 to 33 average backers.

05Projects with a higher number of backers are more likely to succeed.

Successful projects average 204.1 backers — well above every other project state.
Suspended 105.3 · Live 68.1 · Cancelled 26.0 · Failed 16.4 average backers.

06Music, Comic, and Dance subcategories post the highest success rates.

Music (Chiptune) — 77.14% success rate, the highest of any subcategory.
Comic (Anthologies) — 74.81%.
Dance (Residencies) — 72.46%.

07Faster goal completion is associated with higher success rates.

Success rate peaks for campaigns that hit their goal within a 6–29 day window (54%–63%), well above the ~35–52% baseline for slower campaigns.

08Projects that meet their funding goal succeed at a dramatically higher rate — a 35+ point difference versus those that don't.

Goal completed (135,290 projects): 99.26% success rate.
Goal not completed (239,563 projects): 0.74% success rate.

09The United States, Hong Kong, United Kingdom, Denmark, and Singapore post the highest project success rates by country.

United States — 37.59% success rate, 109,238 successful projects.
Hong Kong — 37.43% success rate, 216 successful projects.
United Kingdom · Denmark · Singapore — 36.16% / 34.03% / 32.90%, with 12,056 / 178 / 359 successful projects respectively.
Insights

Insight summary

  • Overall, Kickstarter success rates have stayed relatively stable over the years, with some fluctuation.
  • The 2014–2015 decline was likely driven by a decrease in both backers and pledged amounts, while the 2016–2017 recovery coincided with increased support.
  • Projects with a higher number of backers tend to have higher success rates — building a strong backer community matters.
  • Categories like Comic, Dance, and Music show consistently high success rates, reflecting their popularity among backers.
  • Faster goal completion is a key success factor, suggesting the value of setting realistic, achievable funding goals.
  • Meeting funding goals significantly raises the odds of success — careful planning and budgeting pay off.
  • The United States, United Kingdom, Hong Kong, Denmark, and Singapore lead in project success, reflecting global interest in crowdfunding.
Recommendations

Steps for choosing a potential project to invest in

Step 01 Goal completion Completion of goals, in terms of both time and money, should be a focus — with particular attention to the amount of money pledged by the crowd.
Step 02 Number of backers Backers are the people who back or support the project — the higher the number of backers, the more likely the project is to succeed, since it already has demonstrated interest.
Step 03 Project category Based on prior-year data, select one of the top 5 subcategories from the most successful category.
Step 04 Country Select from the top 5 countries that produce the most successful projects.
